\indent The campfire was dying. The valley was suspiciously quiet -- only a distant stream whispered something to itself. A sickle of moon hung high, pale and cold. Swaromysł sat motionless, hunched under the weight of his own thoughts. Every gust of wind stabbed into his neck, reminding him of what he had left behind. 
\par\noindent-- The fire's dying -- Old Man muttered, dragging out the hissing "s" -- You want us to freeze here? 
\par\indent He sat leaning against a tree trunk, head slightly tilted, as if listening to something he could not see. The scars on his face formed tangled lines, and his milky eyes reflected the last bits of ember. 
\par\noindent-- All right, all right -- Swaromysł looked at him with a faint smile. -- I don't want you to freeze, but if we make it blaze like a witch-burning, someone will spot us. We have to be careful, Old Man. You know we can't let anyone find us here. 
\par\noindent -- Bullshit... -- Old Man snorted, adjusting his fur coat. -- But look at the Boy. He's chattering his teeth so loud half the Valley's gonna hear us. 
\par\indent The Boy flinched, as if something suddenly sobered him. 
\par\noindent-- I... I'm trying -- he whispered, pushing his hands deeper under his arms. -- It's just cold. 
\par\indent Swaromysł covered him with his cloak. Warmth quickly seeped under the fabric, loosening the Boy's tense shoulders. For a moment he only breathed, as if trying to pull himself together. 
\par\noindent-- I know, lad, I know -- Swaromysł murmured. -- But if the Zbuks find us, it'll be worse than cold. 
\par\indent He carefully added wood to the fire. After a moment he felt the Boy's gaze fixed on his scarred forearms. Swaromysł blew on the embers, letting the fire flare up again. He felt the warmth on his face, pleasantly heating his skin. Only then did he notice Old Man watching him with a faint smile. 
\par\noindent-- What happened to your arms? -- the Boy asked, this time more boldly, staring at his forearm. -- You've got scars. 
\par\noindent-- This? -- Swaromysł muttered, as if he didn't feel like returning to the topic. -- Old things. 
\par\indent Old Man snorted louder, with a clear hiss. 
\par\noindent-- Oooold... 
\par\noindent-- Don't worry, Boy. Nothing serious. I just had bad luck once. 
\par\noindent-- Come on, tell me -- the Boy insisted, staring at the scars. -- How did it happen? 
\par\indent Swaromysł was silent for a moment, as if wondering whether he should answer at all. Finally he sighed and leaned against the tree trunk. 
\par\noindent-- Lad... -- he murmured, watching a spark rise from the fire -- This isn't a story for nights like this. 
\par\indent But the Boy didn't look away. Old Man snorted, as if to say "Go on, tell him." Swaromysł sighed heavily. 
\par\noindent-- Fine... -- he said quietly. -- If it bothers you that much. 
\par\indent He stared into the fire for a moment, as if searching for the right words. 
\par\noindent-- You don't start with the scars -- he murmured at last. -- You start with her. 
\par\noindent-- With who? -- the Boy asked, slightly puzzled. 
\par\noindent-- With the Doe -- he answered softly. -- But not the kind you meet in the forest. 
\par\indent Old Man snorted, as if he already knew the story. He leaned more comfortably against the tree, closing his eyes. The Boy kept staring at the fire, listening intently. 
\par\noindent-- With the one who came to me, though she shouldn't have. -- He paused -- And left, though I tried to keep her. 
\par\indent The Boy raised his eyebrows, and Old Man shifted slightly, breaking the silence. 
\par\noindent-- You tried to fucking hold back a being that didn't belong to this world... -- he snorted -- And then you wonder why you've got scars on your arms? 
\par\indent Swaromysł looked at him with a faint smile. 
\par\noindent-- I was young, stupid, naive. Thought I could change her nature. -- he said quietly, not taking his eyes off the fire. 
\par\noindent-- The Doe... -- he began slowly. -- She appeared just like that. No warning. She was like light between trees, like warm wind on a cold day. Calm, safe. I didn't know something like her could exist. She was like a dream that doesn't want to end. But dreams... dreams always end. 
\par\noindent-- That dream lasted long enough, if you ask me -- Old Man muttered, interrupting him. -- But it had to end. And it did. 
\par\noindent-- Yeah... that's how it was -- Swaromysł sighed. -- Though not entirely. Because when it ended, I couldn't keep her. And I tried... I tried everything. The ropes were strong, new. I wrapped them around my forearms to hold her when she started leaving. 
\par\noindent-- But she didn't let you -- the Boy interrupted, staring into the fire. -- And she broke free? 
\par\noindent-- She pulled, Boy. Once. Twice. And then she tore free so hard the ropes ripped my arms down to the bone. And she left. To the sea. Because the sea was her home, not me. She returned to her own. -- he added quietly -- And I stayed. Wounded... bleeding... not understanding what had happened. And I stayed like that for too long. But the body heals, slowly. And so it did. My arms grew over with scars that will remind me of what I lost. 
\par\noindent-- You must've hated her -- the Boy whispered. 
\par\noindent-- Hated? -- Swaromysł looked at him with a faint smile. -- No, Boy. I couldn't hate her. Because when she left, she gave me something I'll never forget. 
\par\noindent-- Ha! Those ugly scars? -- Old Man snorted. -- Don't worry, they'll fade someday. 
\par\noindent-- No, Old Man. That's not it. -- Swaromysł looked at him with an empty gaze. -- By tearing free, she gave me momentum. I started running forward, and nothing and no one can stop me now. If not for her, I'd still be in that place. But now... I go on. 
\par\noindent-- Sad story, Swaromysł -- Old Man muttered, as if to say "Not worth it." -- But not sadder than the other one. Tell him how you lost your eyes. 
\par\noindent-- I can't, Old Man. -- Swaromysł smiled faintly. -- That's a story I don't want to remember. 
\par\noindent-- How did you lose your eyes? -- the Boy asked, confused. -- You're not blind. You have eyes, you see! What happened? 
\par\indent Swaromysł sighed heavily. He stared into the fire, crackling softly as if trying to drown out the silence. The Boy sat still, staring at him with slight confusion. Old Man pulled out his pipe, tapping it lightly against the tree. 
\par\indent And then the ground trembled. Slightly, but enough for Swaromysł to feel it in his bones. He stood slowly, as if wanting to check what was happening. The Boy and Old Man stood too, staring into the darkness. In the distance, branches cracked and leaves rustled. 
\par\noindent-- Oh-ho -- Old Man muttered, still cleaning his pipe. -- Network overload. Third one today. 
\par\indent The Boy looked at him with confusion, and Swaromysł frowned. 
\par\noindent-- Overload? 
\par\indent Swaromysł pointed down the Valley. -- Look. 
\par\indent The Boy turned and froze. Above the geomantic lines running across the valley floor, something began to rise -- something that hadn't been visible before. A light, floating geomantic dust, trembling in the air like dust in a beam of light. 
\par\indent-- It's always there. At rest it blends with the air -- only an Overload shakes it loose. The Network doesn't like you looking at its treasures. 
\par\indent-- Not because it feels anything. That's just how it works. -- Old Man muttered. -- Don't overthink it. 
\par\indent-- I've never seen anything like this... like the Valley is breathing. A bit scary, but beautiful. 
\par\indent-- It'll vanish soon -- The Network doesn't like showing its mistakes. -- Swaromysł said, sitting back down. -- Tomorrow I'll unpack the Latak and start collecting the dust. Technomages will kill for any amount -- without it their machines fall apart. I know what I'm talking about, I used to work with it. 
\par\indent-- Latak? You fly? -- the Boy asked. 
\par\indent-- Yes, but I'll show you tomorrow. For now we need to dim the fire. Zbuks will come out to feed. Overloads activate them. No wonder the dust is so expensive. 
\par\indent-- Fucking Technomages, they had to mess with the Network -- Old Man hissed, pushing burning branches aside. -- Hear that? They're coming. -- he pointed at a swarm of red pulses chaotically darting above the slowly fading, scattered glow of geomantic dust. 
\par\indent-- Let's move. Quickly. -- Swaromysł said, lifting the awkward crates containing the Latak. -- I'm not sitting here like meat on a stick. -- he threw the crate over his shoulder. 
\par\indent-- I see them... gods... -- the Boy whispered, feeling his legs weaken. He staggered as the ground jerked beneath him again, resonating metallically, as if something beneath the Valley was trying to break free. 
\par\indent-- Congratulations, now move your ass unless you want to lose it. -- Old Man urged him, extinguishing the fire. -- I'm not letting some lousy Zbuks get me, move! 
\par\indent Hundreds of red pulses dove at the floating dust above the lines. Like flying piranhas they tore into the silvery cloud. 
\par\noindent-- We need to get off open ground -- into the forest. -- Swaromysł said calmly. -- They'll start looking for meat soon. 
\par\indent He pointed at the dark strip of forest on the ridge -- the only place where they could hide from the Zbuks' sight. They ran. Old Man limped ahead, as if he had known the mountain paths for years. Swaromysł followed, carrying all the gear. The Boy brought up the rear, looking back and stumbling. 
\par\indent The path grew steeper, covered with a thin layer of soil through which hundreds of intertwined roots broke. The sound of Zbuk wings faded, blocked by the curtain of trees and shrubs that cut off the starlight. The Boy tripped and fell to his knees as the ground jerked a second time -- stronger, deeper, as if something in the Valley's bowels had just awakened. 
\par\noindent-- Zbuk! -- Old Man froze mid-step, as if someone had cut off his breath. -- Don't move! 
\par\indent The forest's silence was broken by a metallic clicking. Old Man crouched quietly, fitting himself into a gap between the tree roots. Swaromysł pulled the Boy close, covering his mouth. They listened. The clicking intensified; the Zbuk must have been moving through the treetops. A silver shadow darted between the branches, a crooked, beaked creature poking its head out from the foliage. Its red, pulsing eyes scanned the ground below, trying to catch the slightest movement. The Zbuk tilted its head, and its beaked maw opened with a soft crack, revealing rows of razor-sharp teeth. 
\par\indent Swaromysł moved the Boy an inch to the side, as slowly as if he were moving a stone, not a person. Old Man slid deeper between the roots, hissing softly. 
\par\indent The Zbuk dragged its beaked maw through the air, as if catching a scent, and its silver blade-like pseudo-feathers rippled in the light of its own eyes. The creature's red pupils twitched, sliding slowly across the ground, the roots, the trunks -- then finally lifting upward, disappearing into the treetops. The clicking moved away by a few meters, still disturbingly metallic. 
\par\indent Old Man emerged from the roots, hissing softly, as if at himself. Swaromysł released the Boy and pointed up the path. They moved, placing their steps as carefully as if each one could betray them. 
\par\indent After several dozen meters the trees thinned, revealing a narrow clearing hidden between rocks. The red lights didn't reach here at all. The air was cooler, and the ground finally stopped trembling. Old Man straightened slowly, as if afraid the forest still listened. 
\par\noindent-- Those eyes, those eyes were terrifying... -- the Boy said -- And you were supposed to tell me about yours! 
\par\indent Swaromysł looked into the darkness above the Valley, searching for something he didn't want to find -- My eyes..? -- he murmured. -- That's not a story you start easily. 
\par\indent-- You see, Boy... -- he said after a moment of thought. -- Some things in this Valley look at you before you manage to look at them. And sometimes they take more than they should. I couldn't pull myself together after the Doe left. I was in terrible places, places many never returned from. The run she set me on took me farther than anyone sensible should go. To places where the Network doesn't work as it should. Where the lines are tangled, some of them cracked... where different beings live between them. 
\par\indent Old Man snorted, this time without mockery -- more like someone who knows those places. With respect, he wiped a single tear from his face. 
\par\noindent-- That's where I found the Crow -- Swaromysł said quietly -- Lonely, lost, and wounded. 
\par\noindent-- Lonely, lost, and wounded -- he repeated, as if each word carried its own weight -- She lay between lines I couldn't yet see. Between the creations of Engineers and Technomancers, completely lost, struggling on the ground, unable to lift herself into flight... 
\par\indent The Boy held his breath, and Old Man looked away, as if he didn't want to revisit those memories. 
\par\noindent-- The Crow was... different -- Swaromysł continued. -- Not like the ones you meet in the forests of the Eastern March or the steppes of New Sarmatia. She had something I never understood, and she couldn't understand me either. It was strange, Boy... She wasn't afraid of me; for a time we traveled together, sharing the joys and sorrows of life. We laughed together and cried together. For a brief moment we were one... 
\par\noindent-- And what happened to her? -- the Boy asked, moved. Old Man snorted, still staring into the darkness at the edge of the clearing. 
\par\noindent-- Happened? Nothing happened, Boy -- Swaromysł answered. -- And that's the worst part. For a time we were one and shared the hardships of life. We helped each other become better at what we were. We grew. And when our time ended, we went our separate ways... 
\par\noindent-- And your eyes..? 
\par\indent Swaromysł didn't answer immediately. He turned his head, as if checking whether the Valley truly wasn't listening. Only then did he sigh -- heavy, deep, like someone returning to a place he shouldn't revisit. 
\par\noindent-- My eyes... -- he began slowly. -- When she left, the Crow tore them out. Not because she hated me. She didn't want to hurt me. She did it because she knew that if I kept looking at the world the way I did then... it would destroy me. I wouldn't be able to go on; I'd stay in that place, mistaking the sky for stars reflected at night in the surface of a pond... And I would never move forward... 
\par\noindent-- So she hurt me, Boy. Consciously. Deliberately. With a love that hurts more than hatred. And she left me there so I could learn to see in my own way. Even if it meant leaving scars that can't be hidden. 
\par\indent Swaromysł fell silent. Not because he ran out of words -- but because everything he had to say had already been said. The Valley listened in silence, as if it wanted to remember the story. The Boy sat motionless, staring at him the way one stares at someone who has returned from places that aren't on any map. 
\par\noindent-- That's why... -- he whispered. -- That's why you look the way you do. 
\par\noindent Swaromysł nodded. 
\par\noindent-- Yes, Boy. And that's why I go on. Because when someone closes your eyes... you must learn to open others. 
\par\noindent Old Man snorted, but this time without mockery -- more like someone who understands the weight of those words. 
\par\noindent--- Enough talking -- he muttered. -- The Valley sleeps. We should too. Tomorrow will be a long day. 
\par\indent Dawn rose over the Valley slowly, as if not wanting to startle it. Swaromysł threw the pack over his shoulder and walked up the path -- without haste, without fear, without expectations. 
\par\noindent-- Thank you for always being with me when I need it most. 
\par\noindent He looked slowly around his small camp. 
\par\noindent He was alone. 
\par\noindent As always. 
\par\noindent Because sometimes you go on not because someone is waiting. 
\par\noindent But because you can't stay anymore.
